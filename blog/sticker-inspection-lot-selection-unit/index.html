Sticker Inspection Lots: Define the Stock and Selection Unit

Published November 28, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Define the population before requesting a sample quantity
  2. Name the unit selected and the feature inspected
  3. Make the intended stock accessible
  4. Keep design coverage separate from lot inference
  5. Preserve changes to the proposed lot and selected stock
  6. Prepare an inspection-ready supply discussion

Before agreeing how many stickers to inspect, identify the stock the inspection decision will cover and the physical unit the inspector will select. One purchase order, a shared carton or matching artwork does not settle that scope. A gift-sticker order can contain several designs, constructions or supplied formats, and the inspection record needs to distinguish the relevant differences.

For a multi-design delivery, prepare a lot-and-access map for the responsible quality parties. Have them confirm the grouping, selection method and acceptance plan before anyone gathers convenient pieces from an open box. The map supports that decision; it supplies no sample-size formula, AQL recommendation or guarantee about uninspected stock.

Define the population before requesting a sample quantity

Start with the proposed inspection lot: the identified stock to which a particular decision would apply. Record what is included, what is outside it and which accepted specification governs the relevant features. Retain the supplier's original references alongside any buyer-created grouping. An internal lot name should not overwrite the meaning of a provider's production, roll or order reference.

NIST's acceptance-sampling overview describes random selection from a lot to support a disposition decision. It also distinguishes that purpose from estimating the lot's quality. A pleasing set of selected examples therefore cannot be presented as a measured defect rate or a lot acceptance result without the appropriate method and record.

Describe known differences in artwork revision, material, finish, supplied format or production reference to the quality owner. Do not automatically divide every color into a separate lot, or combine everything because it shares a purchase order. Ask which differences matter to the intended inspection and how the agreed plan treats them. A defensible common grouping may be possible; its basis needs confirmation.

Name the unit selected and the feature inspected

A peelable sticker, complete backing sheet, sealed retail pack and shipping carton are different physical units. The feature being inspected may sit at another level. A pack-contents question involves the complete assortment; a wording question concerns the identified artwork within it. Counting several printed pieces on one selected sheet is not automatically equivalent to selecting several independent sheets.

Give each inspection question an explicit selection unit and observation unit where they differ. Have the quality owner confirm how the plan handles that structure. Do not convert a sample quantity between pieces, sheets and packs merely by multiplying the artwork count.

The print acceptance guide defines the feature and controlling reference. The present task is different: identify the population and physical units from which an agreed inspection can be performed. An accepted visual standard does not answer that mapping question.

Make the intended stock accessible

A list can describe a complete delivery while the inspector can reach only the top bundle. Record that mismatch before describing the selection as representative. Ask how the agreed method will locate and retrieve units across the defined stock without substituting whatever happens to be easiest to reach.

Minitab's attributes-sampling guidance discusses random selection representative of the lot, homogeneous lots and packaging that permits sample selection. These are method considerations, not a decision that every sticker design can be pooled or a requirement that ZC use that software. Obtain the actual project's instructions from the responsible quality parties.

For a gift-sheet range, the access map might connect each artwork version and supplied reference to its labeled bundles and cartons. This is an illustrative arrangement, not a real order. If those associations are missing, request clarification before choosing an inspection grouping from appearance alone. The receiving guide explains preserving original and buyer-created identifiers during repacking.

Keep design coverage separate from lot inference

A buyer may want to see every illustration even when a formal inspection plan has a different selection procedure. Make those two activities visible. Use an identified design-review set for the artwork coverage and retain the selection record required by the agreed lot plan. Do not quietly replace a selected piece with a better-looking example or add convenient pieces and call the result the original random sample.

The multi-design proof plan helps decide which artwork and physical questions each reference represents. A proof set can be valuable without being a sample drawn from the delivered lot. Conversely, a selected delivery sample does not necessarily give every design its own detailed artwork review. If both decisions are needed, have the owners coordinate them explicitly.

Preserve changes to the proposed lot and selected stock

Record actual withdrawals and their original locations. If selected pieces are applied, peeled or otherwise altered, retain that history and the plan's instructions for handling them. A replacement selection, missing unit or inaccessible bundle needs an identified decision; it should not disappear from the report.

Keep a questioned portion, a proposed replacement delivery or later-added stock identifiable. Ask whether the existing plan still applies when the population changes. Repacking alone does not create a common production history, and removing a questioned group does not automatically establish that the remainder is acceptable. The responsible parties decide the scope and disposition under the actual agreement.

If the immediate purpose is to investigate a specific visible issue, use the defect evidence record and retain targeted examples as such. An investigation sample answers a different question from a formal lot decision. Neither route authorizes a buyer to invent an acceptable defect threshold after seeing the results.

Prepare an inspection-ready supply discussion

For custom sticker sheets, connect the artwork versions, complete sheet unit and any retail packing to the proposed lot map. Confirm which identification and packing arrangements can actually be supplied. A custom inspection service, formal plan or third-party attendance needs separate project confirmation; this guide promises none.

Sample availability, charges and timing follow the project-specific sample policy. If no formal lot decision is needed, a clearly bounded receiving check may be the practical alternative. Keep its narrower conclusion explicit rather than giving it a statistical label.

Send the design/revision list, supplied unit and quantities, known stock references, packing map, features and controlling references, agreed inspection requirements and any access gaps in an inspection-scope enquiry. Ask the supplier and quality owner to confirm the proposed lot boundary, selection unit and available handoff before the inspection is scheduled.
