Custom process

How a Custom Sticker Order Moves From Artwork to Production

The useful process is a sequence of decisions, not a promise based on a product photo. Artwork, size, quantity, use condition, format, finish and packing must align before production details are confirmed.

Start With Your RequirementsRead the Factory Ordering Guide

Six steps

A Clear Order Review

  1. 1. Define the jobExplain the product, surface, audience, quantity and how the sticker will be supplied or sold.
  2. 2. Review the artworkCheck file version, final size, cutline, border, small text and material-dependent effects.
  3. 3. Confirm the structureChoose individual die-cut pieces, kiss-cut stickers, sheets, rolls or packed retail sets.
  4. 4. Align the quoteMake sure material, finish, quantity, packing and destination are included in the same specification.
  5. 5. Review proof or sampleUse a digital proof, material reference or physical sample according to the actual project risk.
  6. 6. Approve production detailsConfirm the accepted artwork version and every specification that affects the finished order.

Buyer responsibility

Information the Buyer Should Confirm

  • Rights to reproduce the submitted artwork
  • Final text, spelling, dimensions and quantity
  • Application surface and destination market
  • Packing, labeling and delivery requirements
  • Any document or compliance request tied to the order

Production review

Questions the Factory Discussion Should Resolve

  • Whether the artwork and cutline fit the chosen format
  • Which material and finish direction suits the use case
  • What proof or sample step is appropriate
  • How finished pieces will be counted and packed
  • Which details remain subject to final confirmation

Approval Should Be Specific

A message such as "looks good" can hide uncertainty. A stronger approval names the artwork version, size, format, material, finish and packing direction being accepted. Anything still pending should remain written into the order discussion instead of disappearing between proof and production.
