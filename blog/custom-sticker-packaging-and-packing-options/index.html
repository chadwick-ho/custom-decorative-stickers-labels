Custom Sticker Packing: Allocate Each Design to Its Destination

Updated October 29, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Keep product contents separate from order allocation
  2. Build one matrix with two reconciliation directions
  3. Choose where the split happens
  4. Turn allocations into an identifiable packed state
  5. Control a late change without losing the old instruction
  6. Keep partial releases separate from the remaining allocation
  7. Give receiving the same unit and version

The order total is correct. The cartons are neatly packed. One shop still receives too many of one design while another location is short. An approved sticker assortment tells the supplier what a product contains; it does not necessarily tell the shipping team how the full order is divided among destinations.

Before releasing custom sticker packing instructions, connect the approved product and counting unit to an allocation table. Check both the total for each design and the total assigned to each destination. Then agree who performs the split and which version controls it. This prevents a correct overall quantity from hiding an unusable distribution.

Keep product contents separate from order allocation

Start with the approved assortment, sheet or individual sticker reference. Record the artwork revision and the unit actually being counted: individual backed pieces, complete sheets, finished packs or another agreed item. A quantity without its unit cannot be safely transferred into a shipping instruction.

The retail sticker pack guide defines the product inside each sellable pack. Keep that accepted definition stable while allocating the order. If pack contents change, return to the product approval rather than hiding the change in a destination spreadsheet.

State whether counting, bagging, bundling and destination separation are included in the quoted delivered scope. Do not assume a printer's agreement to supply stickers also includes assembling retail sets or making separate shipments. Obtain confirmation of the actual arrangement.

Build one matrix with two reconciliation directions

Use a row for each approved design or pack version and a column for each delivery allocation. Put the controlling unit in the header. If destinations need different units, keep a clear conversion record instead of adding pieces and packs into the same total.

Allocation field What to check
Design or pack reference Matches the approved artwork and contents
Destination reference Identifies the receiving location without ambiguity
Quantity at that location Uses the stated finished unit
Row total Reconciles that design across all locations
Destination total Reconciles the items assigned to that location
Unallocated balance Has an explicit owner and disposition

Check both directions. A row can total correctly while its destination split is wrong. A destination total can look plausible while the design mix is wrong. Have the business owner approve the actual quantities; the label supplier should not infer demand from store size or previous shipments.

Choose where the split happens

Compare supplier allocation with delivery to a central point followed by local separation. Supplier allocation may reduce work at the central receiving operation when the plan is stable. Central allocation may preserve flexibility when destination needs are still changing. These are considerations to price and confirm, not promised savings.

Ask which tasks and costs each quotation covers: separate packing, identification, storage, additional delivery arrangements or work left for the buyer. The feasible route depends on the actual provider and order. Do not imply that ZC Labels has agreed to multiple shipments, stockholding or a fixed allocation service before confirmation.

If destinations are not ready, separate settled production information from the unresolved logistics decision. Determine with the supplier which details must be fixed before the relevant work begins. Avoid releasing a final allocation by accident because a preliminary table was attached to an approved artwork email.

Turn allocations into an identifiable packed state

Agree whether a carton may mix designs and how those groups remain recognizable. Identify the contents in the same units used by the allocation record. A decorative brand name on a bag does not tell receiving which plan version assigned it to that destination.

Use recipient-supplied identification requirements where applicable. Do not invent a warehouse barcode, carrier label or customer reference because a mockup needs to look complete. The responsible logistics owner supplies actual addresses and required identifiers through the appropriate order channel.

Review the packed configuration where it affects the product. Include the sleeve, backing card, bundle and outer carton used in the proposed scope. Check whether sheet corners, protruding shapes or printed faces need a different arrangement. A neat photograph is not evidence of transit protection; any required transport assessment needs its own agreed method and owner.

The carton identification guide covers package-level identity and visibility. Keep that decision distinct from this allocation matrix, which decides what each destination receives.

Control a late change without losing the old instruction

Give the allocation plan a revision and named owner. When a location quantity changes, reconcile the affected row, destination total and remaining balance. Record which earlier instruction it replaces and obtain confirmation from the party doing the split before assuming the change is operational.

An illustrative buyer might move some finished packs from Location A to Location B while keeping the order total unchanged. That still changes the packing work and may affect labels or shipment arrangements. A message saying “total unchanged” is not a complete revised instruction.

If packing has already begun, ask what can still change and what consequences the supplier can confirm. Do not promise free repacking or an unchanged dispatch date. Preserve accepted stock and unresolved allocations as distinct statuses until the responsible parties agree the next step.

Keep partial releases separate from the remaining allocation

If the parties agree to release only part of an allocation, retain the original approved plan and identify the partial release separately. Do not overwrite a destination's original quantity with its remaining balance: the next person would lose the basis for checking what has already moved. Ask the supplier and logistics owner what information they can confirm and at which event.

Keep separate fields for the planned allocation, quantity authorized for a named release, quantity actually dispatched, quantity actually recorded at receipt and the remaining quantity awaiting a decision. An authorization is not evidence of dispatch; a dispatch is not evidence of receipt or acceptance. Use the same artwork/pack version and counting unit on every row.

Release fieldDecision or evidence to retain
Release identifier and plan revisionWhich instruction and quantity were authorized?
Design/pack version and destinationWhich allocation is affected?
Dispatch recordWhat quantity is actually reported sent under that release?
Receiving referenceWhat quantity was recorded at the destination, using which method?
Remaining allocationWhat is still pending, and who controls its disposition?

In an illustrative plan, Location A is allocated 300 complete sheets of one layout. A first release authorizes 120 sheets. If dispatch of 120 is confirmed, 180 remain undispatched against that allocation; the receiving figure must still be recorded separately when it becomes known. If dispatch confirmation is missing, describe 120 as authorized rather than sent. If the allocation changes, reconcile it against the releases already confirmed instead of applying the new total to every older document.

A partial release can add packing, identification, freight or coordination work. Obtain confirmation of available arrangements, timing and charges before selecting it; this guide does not promise storage or multiple shipments from ZC Labels. Compare it with one complete delivery followed by a buyer-controlled split. Keep the chosen approach linked to the dispatch and receiving milestones and preserve unresolved balances explicitly.

Give receiving the same unit and version

Retain the accepted product reference, allocation revision, packed-state reference and actual receiving information together. If a discrepancy appears, record the affected design, destination and stated unit before comparing it with the order total. Keep the evidence identifiable for the supplier's review.

Allocation happens before receiving: it provides the instruction against which the delivered design quantities can be checked. For complete-sheet orders, keep the custom sticker sheet product route connected to the approved layout and the unit used in the allocation table.

Send ZC Labels a packing and allocation brief with the approved artwork or pack list, quantities in named units, destination allocation, mixing rules, buyer-supplied identification requirements and the delivered scope you want quoted. Those inputs let the supplier confirm what it can pack and separate, while keeping uncertain shipment arrangements visible.
