Automatic Label Applicator Trials: What the Buyer Must Record

Published October 04, 2026. Updated October 21, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Define what the trial is allowed to approve
  2. Identify the machine that actually ran
  3. Tie every observation to a condition
  4. Include the transitions that the job will encounter
  5. Keep a practical alternative in the discussion
  6. Release a bounded specification, not a promise

The trial ends with a neatly labeled bottle on the meeting table. The buyer approves the roll. Later, nobody can say which machine setting produced that bottle, whether it was made before or after an adjustment, or whether the final label construction was even used.

For automatic label applicator setup, I would ask for a reproducible trial record before accepting the attractive piece. The purchasing decision is whether the proposed roll and package have enough relevant application evidence to release the bulk specification. A good-looking hand-applied sample answers a different question.

Define what the trial is allowed to approve

Write down the actual roll construction, artwork revision, container version and intended application method. Identify the line owner who will confirm machine suitability and the buyer who will accept the finished appearance. Ask what remains outside this trial, such as extended storage, later wet exposure or a different container supplier.

An unrepresentative trial can still teach the team something. For example, a temporary artwork file may help explore placement, but it does not approve the readability of the final barcode or longest variant name. State the gap rather than letting the sample acquire a broader meaning as it moves through email.

Before the run, agree which observations matter: missed labels, skew, wrinkles, liner breaks, placement relative to a seam, or the required scan. The responsible owners must define acceptance. Do not invent a universal reject rate, trial length or machine speed to make the record look complete.

Identify the machine that actually ran

An applicator family name may cover different configurations. Record the actual model and relevant fitted components, including the label sensor where applicable. Attach the line's approved roll requirements and the applicable operating reference. Keep machine adjustments and safe operation with trained line personnel.

HERMA's HERMA 500 specification page lists multiple configurations and sensor types. That is useful evidence that equipment identity needs more detail than “automatic applicator.” Its published dimensions, speeds and certifications are specific to that equipment and are not specifications or claims for ZC Labels.

The unwind and core guide explains how to prepare the roll drawing. Confirm that the roll loaded for the trial matches it. If the team substitutes a different core, liner or label gap for convenience, record the substitution and its approval limit.

Tie every observation to a condition

Use a simple record with enough identity to investigate a change:

Trial recordWhy procurement needs it
Roll and artwork identificationConnect observations to the construction being ordered
Container identificationAvoid approving one bottle and ordering for another
Machine and relevant configurationPreserve the application context
Operating conditions recorded by the line teamExplain when an observation occurred
Adjustment and reasonDistinguish the original setup from a corrected one
Retained sample and photo referenceLet another person examine the actual result
Open issue and approval ownerPrevent an unresolved problem disappearing into the bulk order

Separate observations from diagnoses. “Label edge lifted beside the seam after this stage” is a usable observation. “The adhesive is weak” needs supporting investigation. The buyer's record should make the next technical question clearer, not decide its answer from one photograph.

Include the transitions that the job will encounter

A steady section of a run may miss problems around loading, restarting or changing a roll. Ask the line owner which transitions are relevant and safe to include in the agreed trial. Keep representative samples identified by stage rather than putting all finished bottles into one anonymous tray.

If the project has several SKUs, identify which artwork, label size and container combinations were actually observed. A successful trial of one combination does not automatically cover another. Prioritize the differences the technical team considers meaningful; this is not a requirement to invent a new test for every color.

Keep a practical alternative in the discussion

If a roll does not dispense consistently, changing the construction may be justified. So may reviewing the setup, label geometry or container presentation with the relevant supplier. Preserve what changed between trials so an apparent improvement has an interpretable basis.

A different liner is not a universal upgrade. The paper-versus-film liner guide sets out the evidence needed for that substitution. Equally, retaining a proven construction may be sensible if an application correction resolves the observed issue within the agreed conditions.

Hand application can be a separately approved route for a suitable project, but do not quietly substitute it for the automated workflow used in the purchasing plan. Labor, placement checks and the supply format need their own review. A successful hand sample does not erase an unresolved machine issue.

Release a bounded specification, not a promise

At the end, state what passed under which recorded conditions, what changed, what remains unresolved and who accepted each responsibility. Keep rejected examples where they help define appearance limits. Save the final construction reference and the relevant line record with the order.

If the roll, package, artwork geometry or machine configuration later changes, decide which evidence must be repeated. The useful approval is narrow enough to be true and specific enough to find during a reorder. It is not a claim that a short trial proves every hour of future production.

Send ZC Labels the application review brief: equipment and roll requirements, container photos and identification, label dimensions and artwork, the proposed construction, observations with stage references, quantities by SKU and destination. Include the line owner's unresolved question. Those inputs help separate a roll-specification discussion from a machine-setup or package-fit issue.
