Sticker Freight Quotes: Confirm the Packed Weight Basis

Published November 18, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Get a packing basis that matches the purchased units
  2. Check the service's chargeable-weight rule
  3. Price a revised carton against the complete delivery

Ask which packed weight and carton dimensions a sticker freight quote uses before comparing its total. A price based on the stickers alone can change when bags, headers, protective packing and cartons are added. Obtain a carton-by-carton packing estimate, identify the transport service and have the logistics provider confirm the weight it will charge against.

Get a packing basis that matches the purchased units

Start with the quantity and supplied format being quoted. Individual backed stickers, retail packs with headers and multi-sheet sets can occupy different space at the same printed-piece count. Confirm the number of finished units inside each proposed carton and which protection is included. A different carton arrangement must still deliver the approved contents in an acceptable condition.

Ask for each carton's outside length, width and height, packed gross weight and number of identical cartons. Keep units beside the measurements. Record whether the figures come from a representative packed sample, the final shipment or an estimate. If the amount is provisional, ask what event will confirm it and how an adjustment will be handled. The sticker quote comparison guide helps check which packing and delivery charges are included.

Check the service's chargeable-weight rule

DHL Express UK's measuring guide compares scale weight with volumetric weight and uses the greater for the parcel charge. Its centimetre-and-kilogram example uses length × width × height divided by 5,000 and describes rounding to the next half kilogram. Have the transport provider confirm the rule for the actual service, route and quotation. DHL Express UK parcel measuring guidance.

For an illustrative carton measuring 40 × 30 × 20 cm, that divisor gives 4.8 kg of volumetric weight. If the packed scale weight is 3 kg, 4.8 kg is the larger figure before service rounding. A 40 × 30 × 10 cm alternative would give 2.4 kg. At the same hypothetical 3 kg scale weight, scale weight would then be larger. These figures explain the calculation. Ask the supplier whether the complete order actually fits either arrangement with suitable protection.

Price a revised carton against the complete delivery

Ask for an updated quotation after a proposed packing change. Compare any extra assembly or material cost, the receiving team's work and the resulting freight amount. Removing a header or splitting a retail set changes the product the buyer receives. If another carton count is proposed, have the provider restate the total using its actual multi-piece rules.

Send artwork and dimensions, quantities by supplied unit, retail packing requirements, destination postal code and receiving window. Include the proposed carton record if one is available. Ask which packing and freight figures can be confirmed for your sticker sheet order under the ZC shipping policy.
