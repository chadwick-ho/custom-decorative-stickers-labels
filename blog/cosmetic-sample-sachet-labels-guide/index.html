Sample Sachet Labels: Keep the Seal and Tear Route Clear

Published 2026-10-03. Updated October 25, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Choose a Real Usable Panel
  2. Keep Claims and Small Type Honest
  3. Trial the Filled, Handled Sachet
  4. Map the unopened and opening states on one reference
  5. Review a final-size piece through normal handling
  6. Choose the information route before shrinking the text
  7. Keep the released footprint with the pack revision

An added label fits inside the sachet’s outer rectangle but approaches its tear notch and seal. Map the filled, sealed sample before authorizing the footprint. An unused strip of artwork space is not automatically an available label panel.

Measure the flat face, then mark heat seals, notch, crimping and the area that bulges after filling. A label placed across a seal edge may lift or interfere with opening. A label placed on the most flexible center may wrinkle. Those are packaging questions to test with the sachet owner, not details to guess from a PDF.

Choose a Real Usable Panel

Heat-sealed edgeIrregular contact or opening interferenceMark a clear setback
Tear notchSticker bridges opening pathOpen an applied sample
Filled centerFlex and label wrinklingHandle a filled sachet
Variable lot areaInformation changes by batchAgree on coding owner and method

An original botanical illustration can distinguish a calming blue variant from a bright citrus one, but the product name needs equal attention. Avoid using color as the only cue: a packer or shopper should still identify the sample under ordinary light. If a label sheet contains several variants, confirm the count and approved artwork version for each.

Keep Claims and Small Type Honest

The cosmetics brand is responsible for ingredient, warning, origin and other market-specific information. Do not invent a clinical claim or certification icon just to fill space. If the sachet cannot hold readable required information, the owner needs to decide whether an outer card, sleeve or different pack format is appropriate. The print proof should use supplied, approved copy.

Check the smallest text at actual physical size on a printed proof. Contrast can change on metallic or translucent film. If the design uses a clear label, test it over the real sachet background; the color underneath may make fine letters disappear. A beautiful enlarged screen render is not a readability test.

Trial the Filled, Handled Sachet

  1. Send filled sachet samples, not only a dieline
  2. Mark seals, notch and flexing areas
  3. Approve all product text and claims by variant
  4. Proof tiny type at final print size
  5. Test label adhesion after normal handling
  6. Open the sachet with the label applied

Map the unopened and opening states on one reference

Use the actual filled, sealed sachet, not only an empty flat pouch drawing. Mark the heat-seal bands, tear notch, intended tear path and the owner's approved area for an added label. A sticker that fits between the outside edges can still run into the opening route. Keep dimensional clearance tied to the actual pack drawing and a physical example; this article supplies no universal safe margin.

Decide which face the recipient sees first and which information must remain readable after opening. If an added sticker covers existing copy, the responsible product owner needs to resolve that information placement. Moving the same sticker to the other face is not an automatic answer when that face carries the only approved instructions or identification.

Review a final-size piece through normal handling

Place a size mock-up on a representative sealed sample and follow the intended opening action. Check that the mock-up does not bridge the notch or obstruct the intended grip. This is a footprint review, not proof of adhesion, seal integrity or product suitability. Those require their own owner-defined scope and appropriate evidence.

Then review a proposed finished label under the agreed conditions. Small flexible packs can change shape as they are held. Ask whether the label remains readable at the relevant stage and whether its placement makes the pack harder to open. Keep the observed package state with the result instead of recording only that the sticker fitted.

Choose the information route before shrinking the text

If the usable area is insufficient, compare a smaller approved information set, an outer carrier or a different packaging print route. An outer card gives more space but may be discarded before use. A separate carton changes the pack and packing task. Direct pouch printing changes the supply route. Each needs approval from the relevant owner; the label supplier should not silently remove information to make the artwork fit.

Review the longest approved product name and any variable reference at final size. Do not use an unreadable line as a placeholder for a problem that belongs with the product owner. A good approval record can say that the label route is unsuitable for this pack, rather than forcing a decorative rectangle into an unusable space.

Keep the released footprint with the pack revision

For another flexible cosmetic package, the tube movement review addresses changing shape rather than a sachet tear path.

For the supply-format discussion, use the packaging label product page with the physical and artwork references above.

Retain the filled-pack reference, seal and opening map, final artwork revision and accepted placement together. A changed notch or seal layout needs another footprint review even if the sachet's overall dimensions remain similar. Send those references, the approved copy, destination and quantities per version through the sample-sachet enquiry. The immediate decision is whether an added label has a usable route on this specific pack, not whether every sachet can take the same sticker.
