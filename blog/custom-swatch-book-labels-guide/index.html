Swatch Labels: Close the Distributed Book Revision

Published 2026-10-04. Updated October 23, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Give Each Physical Sample a Stable Identity
  2. Test Handling and Replacement
  3. Keep the Book Current
  4. Link the swatch to a material revision, not only a name
  5. Make retirement visible in the sample system
  6. Separate identity printing from appearance approval
  7. Reconcile the issued books, not just the central file

The central swatch list is current, but a sales book at another location may still contain the retired material. Reconcile the distributed physical books as well as the artwork. A label revision becomes useful when the material piece, its code and the recipient’s completed change describe the same offering.

Start by identifying where the label belongs: on a backing card, a plastic tab, a sample envelope or the swatch itself. Directly sticking to fabric or a coated finish can alter the material or make removal difficult. The collection owner should decide whether the sample will be reordered, replaced or discarded after use.

Give Each Physical Sample a Stable Identity

FieldPurposeApproval question
Swatch nameHuman recognitionExact approved spelling
SKU or material codeQuotation and reorderUnique current identifier
Collection versionSeparates old from newWho updates the book?
Original color cueVisual navigationDoes it compete with the sample?

An original geometric icon can distinguish a family of materials, but the real swatch must remain visible. A decorative sticker should not cover the exact texture or color the buyer is evaluating. Use a consistent placement and leave enough room for the longest plausible code, not just the shortest sample in the first batch.

Test Handling and Replacement

Make a short prototype book. Turn pages repeatedly, carry it in a bag and let several people pull sample tabs as they normally would. Check whether label edges catch, type rubs away or a code disappears behind a binder ring. If swatches are frequently replaced, trial removal from the chosen backing card or tab without tearing it.

Printed color on a label is not a guaranteed match to the physical textile, paint or material. Avoid presenting the small sticker as a color standard unless the collection owner has a controlled approval method. The sample itself is the reference; the label supports identity and navigation.

Keep the Book Current

  1. Send the actual swatch holder or backing card
  2. Supply approved names, codes and version rules
  3. Proof the longest code at final size
  4. Test repeated page turns and sample removal
  5. Check labels near binder rings and cutouts
  6. Define how retired samples and labels are replaced

Link the swatch to a material revision, not only a name

Two samples can share a marketing color name while referring to different material revisions. Define which record the label identifies: material code, revision, collection issue and any owner-approved availability status. Keep that record connected to the physical sample. A color name alone may help a conversation but cannot establish what purchasing should quote or reorder.

The collection owner should decide which changes require a new identifier and which can retain an existing code. The label supplier should not invent revision meanings. Ask for an example of a current sample and a replaced one so the intended distinction is visible before the whole collection is printed.

Make retirement visible in the sample system

Decide what happens when a material is discontinued or a sample no longer represents the current offering. Remove or clearly control the retired reference through the collection owner's process. Putting a new sticker over the old name can preserve a misleading physical sample even if the text is current. Replacement may need to involve both the material piece and its label.

Keep a small change record that connects old code/revision, new code/revision and the action taken in the book. If sample books exist at several sales locations, identify how the change reaches them. A new central spreadsheet does not update the physical book already on a customer's desk. This is a proposed control, not a claim about an existing ZC Labels system.

Separate identity printing from appearance approval

The printed label can carry an original color cue for navigation, but it should not silently become the color standard for the underlying fabric or finish. Have the collection owner identify the controlling physical reference and any agreed appearance-approval method. Different lighting or a printed swatch icon should not redefine the material being sold.

Check the longest code and revision together at final size. Place the label on the actual card, tab or holder, keeping the material's inspection area clear. Open and close the prototype book and check access around rings, folds and pockets. A well-controlled code still needs to remain readable after normal handling.

If a separate removable sample is supplied, ensure its identity travels with it. An empty holder labeled correctly does not identify the fabric that was pulled out and passed around. Compare backing cards, attached tabs or other owner-approved methods according to how the sales team actually uses the collection.

When quoting from a detached sample, ask the sales team to carry its code and revision into the enquiry. A photograph of a blue fabric without its identity may be useful for discussion but cannot establish a reorder. The controlled reference prevents appearance memory from silently selecting another material.

Reconcile the issued books, not just the central file

Give each distributed sample book or controlled collection issue a reference the owner can track. A corrected central spreadsheet does not identify which physical books received the replacement material and matching label. The practical question is which issued references still represent the current offering.

Prepare a change instruction that names the affected material code/revision, the required physical action and the intended replacement. Replacing a label alone is insufficient where the material piece itself changed. The collection owner should define the permitted status for books that cannot yet be updated or located.

Book state Evidence to retain
Change issued Identified book and required material/label revision
Completed Confirmation of the physical replacement or retirement
Pending Open action and responsible recipient
Unavailable Unconfirmed status, without assuming completion

Ask for completion against the actual change, not simply confirmation that an envelope arrived. A recipient may have received new labels while the old swatch remains in its holder. An identified photograph or another owner-agreed record can support reconciliation, but its scope should be clear; it does not prove every other page was reviewed.

When an enquiry comes from a book with unconfirmed status, check the quoted material code and revision before treating its physical appearance as the current sales reference. Do not automatically reject the enquiry or assert that the sample is wrong. Establish which reference the buyer saw and ask the collection owner to resolve the difference.

A distributed replacement program adds coordination work, so compare it with replacing a complete issue where appropriate. A whole new book can simplify revision identity but may consume more material and leave old books in circulation. A targeted change uses fewer new pieces while demanding accurate recipient completion. Keep that tradeoff visible rather than claiming one route is always cheaper or more sustainable.

Send material/revision examples, book-issue identifiers, required physical replacement, holder photos, longest fields and quantities in a swatch-label enquiry. These distinguish a central artwork change from a distributed book update.
