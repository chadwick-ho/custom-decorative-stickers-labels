Custom Tea Tin Labels: Make the Lid, Body and Refill Work Together

Published October 17, 2026. Practical production planning for custom sticker buyers.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Map the Two Viewing Positions
  2. Plan for the Next Fill
  3. Questions Before You Order

A tea tin looks orderly in a row until someone stacks the tins. Then only the lids are visible. Move them to a shelf and the body becomes the selling face. I would ask how the tins are stored before deciding which panel carries the tea name. The lid and body do not need identical artwork, but they do need to agree.

Map the Two Viewing Positions

Put finished tins in the intended display: stacked, side by side or inside a gift box. Mark the available flat lid area and straight body height. Check whether a rolled edge, closure ridge or vertical metal seam interrupts the label. A round lid proof should sit centered after the lid is fully closed, not only when it is loose on a table.

Use complete original leaf or botanical illustrations to build a family, then make the tea type readable in more than color alone. A worker picking two similarly green tins needs a clear word, not an almost invisible hue difference. If the tin is a gift, check that any ribbon or sleeve does not cover the only product identity.

Plan for the Next Fill

Some tins are sold once; others are meant to be refilled. If the latter, decide what remains permanent and what must change with the contents. A decorative brand label could stay on the body while an owner-approved variant and batch label changes on the lid. Trial removal and relabeling on the final coated metal. Do not promise clean removal from a material name alone.

Check the label after normal hand handling, lid opening and any expected kitchen moisture. The food business must approve product description, ingredients and market-specific wording. The supplier can help choose space and legibility, but should not invent a tea claim to fill the panel.

  • Photograph the intended shelf and stack
  • Measure lid and straight body areas
  • Check seams, rolled edges and closure clearance
  • Proof all variants on closed tins
  • Define keep-or-replace for refill use
  • Verify approved copy by tea type

The tea packaging guide covers wider format choices. For reuse, compare the removable and permanent label guide. Send tin dimensions and the display photo through the quote form.

Questions Before You Order

Should the product name go on the lid or the tin body?

Use the real display and storage position to decide. If tins stack, the lid matters; if they face outward on a shelf, the body must identify the tea.

Can a tea tin label be changed for refills?

That depends on the brand workflow and chosen construction. Define whether a permanent decorative panel stays and a variable identity label changes.

Can one label size fit every tea tin?

Only after measuring each tin and checking curvature, seams and lid clearance on actual samples.
