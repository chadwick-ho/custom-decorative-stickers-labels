Custom Tea Packaging Labels for Tins and Pouches

Published September 25, 2026. A factory-side guide for loose-leaf tea, sachet and gift-collection projects.

By ZC Labels Production Team · Reviewed for B2B sticker sourcing.

Tea packaging often begins with mood: quiet mountains, garden flowers, bright citrus or a traditional brewing ritual. That is useful creative material, but the production conversation begins somewhere more ordinary. Is the label going onto a rigid tin, a paper pouch, a laminated stand-up bag or a box?

The same artwork can behave very differently on those surfaces. A beautiful wide label may bridge a pouch gusset. A small round seal may sit well on a tin lid but add little information to the body. We first match the label structure to the pack, then let the visual story grow inside that boundary.

Tins and Pouches Need Separate Decisions

A cylindrical tin needs a measured straight wall and a deliberate seam. A rectangular tin needs corner clearance. A flexible pouch changes shape when filled, and its zipper, seal area, folds and gussets should stay clear. Measure the filled pouch rather than an empty flat film sample.

If the pouch already has a high-barrier construction, the label should not be described as the reason the tea stays fresh. The package and closure do that work. The label provides identification, information and presentation.

Original Artwork Can Carry Origin and Flavor

Tea does not need blank labels to avoid infringement. Original jasmine flowers, bergamot fruit, mint leaves, tea terraces, ceramic cups and abstract steam patterns can give a range a clear visual voice. These elements should describe the specific product honestly rather than imitate the symbols or layout of a famous tea house.

We like to keep the brand block, tea name, type and net-content area stable. Illustration and accent color can change by SKU. A mountain black tea can use deep green and a ridge drawing; a citrus blend can use warm yellow and an original peel illustration. The range stays related without becoming repetitive.

Make Brewing Information Easy to Use

Brewing guidance is practical content, not a decorative paragraph. Temperature, amount and time should be readable and grouped. Simple original icons can help, but the numbers need enough contrast and size to work on the final package.

Ingredient, allergen, origin, organic and certification statements require brand-side approval and appropriate records. A landscape illustration can express place as a design theme; it should not become an unsupported geographic or certification claim.

Test the Label on a Filled Pack

Fill the pouch or tin to its normal volume. Apply the label using the planned hand or machine method. Open and close the package repeatedly, stack it with neighboring packs and place it in the intended gift box or shipping carton. Watch for edge lift, wrinkles, abrasion and information hidden by the expanded shape.

For paper-like materials, also consider how the surface responds to kitchen moisture and hand oils. For high-gloss film, check whether shelf glare hides the tea name. The finish should support both the story and the use environment.

Our Tea Packaging Test List

  • Identify the exact tin, pouch or carton construction
  • Measure usable space on a filled package
  • Keep clear of zippers, seals, folds, gussets and tin edges
  • Print the tea name and brewing guide at actual size
  • Compare every botanical illustration and SKU color
  • Apply labels with the planned packing workflow
  • Run opening, closing, stacking and carton-abrasion checks
  • Confirm all ingredient, origin and certification wording

What We Would Put in the Quote Request

Send the final package or specification, filled dimensions, surface description, label position, artwork, number of teas, quantities by SKU, application method, finish direction and gift-box or carton requirements. Tell us which information changes between blends.

A useful tea label gives the blend a visual world while keeping the buying decision simple. The package protects aroma; the label earns recognition through clear information and original, relevant artwork.
