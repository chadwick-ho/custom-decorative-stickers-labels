Custom Stickers for Product Packaging: Map the Range Before Ordering

Updated October 29, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Separate the package from the surface the label touches
  2. Make one row for each real application
  3. Keep application conditions separate from later use
  4. Decide where a common construction is worth reviewing
  5. Choose the delivered format around the packing task
  6. Link each approval to the right question
  7. Send one range brief with separate rows

The same brand artwork is going on a carton, a jar and a flexible pouch. Procurement asks for one packaging sticker specification so the project stays simple. The supplier asks what each surface actually is and when the labels will be applied. Those are the questions that determine whether a common construction is a useful proposal or an assumption that hides three different jobs.

For custom stickers for product packaging, map each package to its contact surface, application step and use conditions before deciding how many specifications to request. A shared artwork family may be sensible. A shared material and adhesive still need confirmation against the actual packages.

Separate the package from the surface the label touches

“Paper box” may mean a printed, coated or laminated carton. A glass jar can have a curved panel, a seam or a label location affected by filling and wiping. A pouch may flex when handled. Describe the area the adhesive contacts, not only the material beneath it.

Send a wide photograph of each package and a closer image of the proposed label area. Mark the panel, folds, seams and openings. Identify the contact material and finish from the packaging supplier when known. A picture is useful for geometry; it does not identify the chemistry of an unfamiliar coating.

Avery Dennison's product-selection considerations identify surface composition, texture and shape as factors in selection. That supports asking about the real contact area. It does not establish that a particular ZC Labels adhesive is suitable or that a generic substrate name is enough to approve it.

Make one row for each real application

Brief field Record for each package
Package and version Actual carton, container or pouch and its owner
Contact area Material or finish, shape, seams and proposed label position
Application When and how the label is applied; relevant conditions
Use afterward Handling, moisture or other contact that actually matters
Artwork and quantity Approved revision, dimensions and finished units per SKU
Decision still open Evidence required and the person who can confirm it

The matrix is a way to organize questions, not a product test or guarantee. If one field is unknown, leave it open and identify the next person to ask. A shorter brief with a visible gap is better than a complete-looking table built from guesses.

Keep application conditions separate from later use

A label may be applied to a clean, dry surface and later meet condensation. Another may be applied at a different stage of filling or packing. Record both stages instead of asking for a material that is simply “strong” or “waterproof.”

If a package is wiped before labeling, ask its owner what preparation is approved for that actual finish. Do not prescribe a solvent from a generic guide. If a label is applied by equipment, the line's roll requirements are another input. Neither surface selection nor an attractive hand-applied sample settles machine compatibility.

The surface-matching guide goes deeper into the contact surface and end state. The range map here gives that later discussion a useful starting point: which package and which conditions the team is actually talking about.

Decide where a common construction is worth reviewing

A common construction can simplify artwork and stock management if it is suitable for each identified job. Separate constructions may be more practical if one package needs a different review, handling method or appearance. Neither choice wins merely because all the labels share a logo.

For an illustrative botanical range, a buyer could compare the same proposed construction on the actual carton and jar while keeping the flexible pouch pending. The comparison must identify the proposed stock, the two package versions and the observations the owners need. It is an illustrative plan, not evidence that those samples passed or that one construction is available from ZC Labels.

If the package finish, label size or handling changes, ask which previous observation still represents the new job. A familiar-looking replacement carton is not necessarily the same contact surface. Record the change rather than diagnosing a failure before it occurs.

Choose the delivered format around the packing task

A roll, sheet or individually backed piece describes how the order arrives. It does not by itself establish adhesion or environmental performance. Identify who applies each label and whether different designs must be picked together or run on separate equipment.

For a detailed allocation comparison, use the roll-versus-sheet guide. A mixed sheet may be useful for some manual tasks; an established applicator may require a roll with a specific presentation. Confirm the actual working method rather than comparing an isolated price for different delivered units.

State quantities by artwork and by finished unit. A total across the range does not show how many labels belong to each package. If the supplier proposes a common size or format, ask what changes at the packing table and which package-specific checks remain open.

Link each approval to the right question

A digital proof can help review artwork, wording and dimensions. A representative physical reference may help with appearance, fit or handling, depending on what it represents. Keep those decisions separate: an approved PDF does not establish application performance on every package.

Ask which sample or other reference the supplier can provide, what construction it represents, any charge and what timing can actually be confirmed. Agree who will judge the relevant result. Do not promise a sample route, test standard, MOQ, price or lead time before the supplier has confirmed the project.

For claims or required product information, use copy approved by the responsible business. A label printer's artwork review is not a determination of legal compliance. If a statement, code or market requirement remains unresolved, identify its owner before releasing the affected proof.

Send one range brief with separate rows

Start the discussion on the custom stickers and decorative labels page. For a packaging sticker enquiry, send the package photographs and versions, contact surfaces, label dimensions, application and use conditions, approved artwork, quantities by SKU and destination.

Mark what is intended to be common and what must differ. Those details let the supplier propose a comparable scope and identify the evidence still needed before the range becomes a production instruction.
