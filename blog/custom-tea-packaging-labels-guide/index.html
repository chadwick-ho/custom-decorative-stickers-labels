Tea Packaging Labels: Separate the Blend from the Pack Format

Published 2026-09-25. Updated October 26, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

  2. Original Artwork Can Carry Origin and Flavor
  3. Make Brewing Information Easy to Use

The tea blend is correct, but the label file belongs to the tin while the order is for a pouch. Keep blend identity and package format as separate references. One attractive range design cannot tell the packing team which footprint and quantity belong to each physical pack.

Tins and Pouches Need Separate Decisions

A cylindrical tin needs a measured straight wall and a deliberate seam. A rectangular tin needs corner clearance. A flexible pouch changes shape when filled, and its zipper, seal area, folds and gussets should stay clear. Measure the filled pouch rather than an empty flat film sample.

If the pouch already has a high-barrier construction, the label should not be described as the reason the tea stays fresh. The package and closure do that work. The label provides identification, information and presentation.

Round metal tinBody wrap plus lid identifierEntering the top or bottom roll edge
Rectangular tinFront panel and small back panelPlacing corners too close to tin edges

Original Artwork Can Carry Origin and Flavor

Tea does not need blank labels to avoid infringement. Original jasmine flowers, bergamot fruit, mint leaves, tea terraces, ceramic cups and abstract steam patterns can give a range a clear visual voice. These elements should describe the specific product honestly rather than imitate the symbols or layout of a famous tea house.

A practical option is to keep the brand block, tea name, type and net-content area stable. Illustration and accent color can change by SKU. A mountain black tea can use deep green and a ridge drawing; a citrus blend can use warm yellow and an original peel illustration. The range stays related without becoming repetitive.

Make Brewing Information Easy to Use

Brewing guidance is practical content, not a decorative paragraph. Temperature, amount and time should be readable and grouped. Simple original icons can help, but the numbers need enough contrast and size to work on the final package.

Ingredient, allergen, origin, organic and certification statements require brand-side approval and appropriate records. A landscape illustration can express place as a design theme; it should not become an unsupported geographic or certification claim.

Test the Label on a Filled Pack

Fill the pouch or tin to its normal volume. Apply the label using the planned hand or machine method. Open and close the package repeatedly, stack it with neighboring packs and place it in the intended gift box or shipping carton. Watch for edge lift, wrinkles, abrasion and information hidden by the expanded shape.

Give the product and package independent identities

Start with the owner-approved blend list. Then list the actual tin, pouch, carton or other format offered for each blend. Connect each valid combination to its artwork, label position and quantity. Avoid a file name such as citrus-final when it conceals whether the output is for a pouch front, tin body or lid piece.

This is not a reason to split one artwork task into several thin buying guides. The existing overview remains the place to coordinate the range. Its job is to make the product-to-format relationships visible before the files are released, while the more focused tin guide answers placement questions after that format is chosen.

Do not assume that every blend is offered in every format. An unused combination should remain absent or explicitly not ordered. Filling a matrix with copied values can create a plausible-looking file that has no actual purchasing instruction behind it.

Count each physical piece in the combination

For an illustrative range, a tin may use a body label and a lid identifier while a pouch uses front and back pieces. The pack counts can match while the piece sizes, orientations and quantities differ. Keep those pieces separate in the ordering record and confirm what is supplied as a set, if anything.

RecordExample meaningCheck before release
Blend referenceThe product being identifiedOwner-approved identity and copy
Package referenceThe actual tin or pouchFilled dimensions and surface
Piece referenceLid, body, front or backCorrect layout and position
Order quantityPieces or complete setsUnits and allocation explicitly named

These are suggested record roles, not a promised supplier stock-management system. Ask which references the supplier can preserve on the delivered packing and what the buyer must control internally. A total written as labels is ambiguous if the parties mean different delivered units.

Review the most demanding combinations

Use the longest blend name and the tightest legitimate panel when planning the first physical review. Then confirm the remaining combinations against their own approved references. A successful short-name tin proof cannot approve the pouch's zipper clearance, and a pouch layout cannot settle where a lid identifier faces in a drawer.

Keep the illustration and product identity coherent, but do not make every format an exact scale copy. The label can change its layout while retaining the same authorized brand system. Compare the cost of another footprint or layout with the operational cost of an unreadable or mismatched package. No numerical savings are assumed.

When a package or blend changes, mark which rows are affected. Retain the accepted references for unaffected combinations instead of reopening the whole range without a reason. Hold the changed row until its contents, footprint and quantity are reconciled. That is more useful than replacing every file with a new final suffix.

Use the tea tin guide once the tin placement is the specific decision. The custom roll label page supports the commercial format discussion.

Send ZC Labels a blend-by-format brief: blend list, actual package references, label positions, approved artwork and copy, quantity per physical piece and destination. Mark unoffered combinations. These inputs allow the next proof to be reviewed as a defined packing instruction rather than an isolated tea illustration.
