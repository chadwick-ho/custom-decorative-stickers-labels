Perforated Roll Labels: Specify the Tear Without Damaging the Label

Published October 03, 2026. Updated October 21, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Name the layer and the separation task
  2. Show the complete hand movement
  3. Balance convenient tearing against web integrity
  4. Do not confuse a tear line with a peel feature
  5. Keep machine use as a separate requirement
  6. Approve the sequence and save the sample

The packing team asks for a roll that tears into single-label segments. The supplier hears “easy peel.” Those requests can lead to different constructions: one separates the backing into manageable lengths; the other helps lift a sticker from its liner.

For perforated roll labels used by hand, I would first specify what must tear, where the tear belongs and what must remain intact. A dotted line in the artwork is not enough. The useful purchase decision is whether a proposed liner perforation works through dispensing, tearing, peeling and applying in the actual packing motion.

Name the layer and the separation task

State whether the request is a perforation in the liner between finished labels or an intentional detachable section through a label. These are different products. A hand-packing request to separate backing should not quietly cut through the brand name or change the finished seal.

Avery's WePrint perforation guidance describes perforation between roll labels within its own service. Its liner restrictions and commercial terms are supplier-specific. They are a reason to ask about the actual proposed construction, not permission to assume the same availability or price from ZC Labels.

Keep the label cutline, liner gap and perforation as distinct annotations. If a tear must occur after a strip of several labels, specify that interval separately from the total labels per roll. Do not let the packer discover the chosen repeat when the carton arrives.

Show the complete hand movement

Ask the packing team to demonstrate how a segment leaves the roll and reaches the package. Record whether the roll rests on a table or holder, which hand supports the package and whether the operator tears before or after peeling. Include normal gloves and the location of the waste container where relevant.

The same perforation can feel different when pulled from a loose roll and from a dispenser holding the web under tension. Review the actual arrangement. A neat torn segment in the supplier's hand does not show whether the next label begins lifting during the buyer's motion.

For an illustrative tea-packing task, the operator may want to pass one backed label to a second station. Tearing the liner can support that task. If the operator simply peels and applies from a fixed holder, a continuous backing may already be convenient. The extra tear is not automatically a benefit.

Balance convenient tearing against web integrity

A perforation weakens a chosen line deliberately. The buyer wants separation when intended while preserving handling before that point. Ask for a representative converted sample and observe it through mounting, ordinary unwinding, segment separation and the next pick.

Do not choose a tooth count, cut depth or tear-force threshold from an illustration. The converter must establish a feasible specification for the liner and geometry, and the buyer needs to agree what acceptable use looks like. A tear that is easy on a desk may still be inconvenient in the actual sequence.

ObservationWhat to record before deciding
Web separates before the intended pullHolder, mounting and unwinding conditions; retain the segment
Operator struggles to start the tearGrip, pull angle and whether both hands are available
Tear approaches the printed labelPerforation position relative to the label edge and gap
Next label lifts or catchesWhat happens immediately after separation, not only at the torn edge
Backed segment reaches another stationIdentification, handling and disposal at that station

These observations guide the next discussion; they are not a diagnosis of the converter's process. Keep the sample reference so the supplier can inspect the actual construction.

Do not confuse a tear line with a peel feature

If the complaint is that the label edge is hard to lift, a liner perforation may leave that problem unchanged. A larger backing margin, a split backing or another feasible handling feature might be worth evaluating. The easy-peel guide separates those choices.

If the problem is mixed designs and frequent selection, supply allocation may matter more than tearing. The roll-versus-sheet guide can help compare the full packing job. A sheet or a different arrangement may remain the better alternative rather than adding another operation to the roll.

Ask which proposed change actually removes the difficult step. Do not order a feature because its name sounds close to the symptom.

Keep machine use as a separate requirement

If the same artwork will later run on an automatic applicator, tell the supplier before approving a hand-packing perforation. Web integrity, sensing and the relevant machine requirements need confirmation. A manually convenient construction is not automatically interchangeable with the line's approved roll.

It may be sensible to maintain separate supply specifications for hand distribution and automatic application. Compare that arrangement against actual quantities, identification and storage needs. Avoid a universal “one roll for everything” instruction that hides incompatible uses.

Record core, winding, outside diameter and relevant roll count as well. The unwind and core guide covers how to state those requirements without relying on a chart number alone.

Approve the sequence and save the sample

Retain the accepted liner/label construction, annotated tear position, separation interval, roll specification and sample reference. Record the holder and packing sequence used in review. If the liner or application route changes on reorder, identify which observations need repeating.

Send ZC Labels the hand-packing brief: a short photo sequence or video of dispensing and applying, finished size and artwork, intended tear position and interval, liner information if known, holder and roll requirements, quantities by SKU and destination. Those details let the discussion distinguish a tear-line request from a different peel or allocation problem.
