Printed Sticker Weathering Reports: Read the Exposure Basis

Published November 23, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Confirm that the tested specimen represents the proposed print
  2. Read the light and moisture conditions
  3. Compare observations against a defined reference
  4. Keep screening and service-life prediction distinct
  5. Close the evidence gap before changing the construction

Before comparing accelerated-weathering hours for printed stickers, read the specimen and exposure behind each result. Identify the finished print stack, the test method and cycle, and the feature judged afterward. A larger hour total is not a ranking when the two reports exposed different constructions in different ways.

The purchase decision is whether supplied evidence can support a comparison of the proposed stickers for the intended use. Keep that separate from predicting years outdoors. The outdoor exposure guide records the installed surface, orientation and environment; use that profile as the question the report must help answer.

Confirm that the tested specimen represents the proposed print

Obtain the report identity and the specimen reference. Connect the artwork revision, face material, printing and protective finish to the proposed construction. Where the question includes attachment, identify the adhesive, mounting surface and application preparation represented in the test.

A raw face-film result is not automatically evidence for the later printed and protected label. A flat coupon may represent a particular print comparison while leaving curved application or edge behavior unresolved. Preserve those limits. Do not add a missing production or mounting detail from the sample’s appearance.

Identify the feature the buyer needs to retain: an acceptable logo color, readable wording, an agreed surface appearance or a particular physical condition. One report can contain several observations. The acceptance owner should say which ones matter to the order before a general “weather resistant” description travels into purchasing copy.

Read the light and moisture conditions

Q-Lab’s introduction to weathering distinguishes lightstability from combined weathering exposure and describes different fluorescent-UV and xenon-arc test architectures. Lamp characteristics, optical filtering where used, moisture and controlled temperature are part of the exposure. A light-only result and a result including wet stages should not silently become the same claim.

Ask the report provider for the applicable method designation and version, apparatus configuration and actual cycle used. Have the technical reviewer identify which lamp or filter information, irradiance settings, temperature definitions and moisture stages are required to interpret that method. This guide supplies no preferred lamp, cycle or numerical exposure setting for a ZC construction.

Compare observations against a defined reference

Retain the report’s stated comparison reference and assessment method. Ask how the important artwork areas were identified and whether observations were visual, instrumental or another specified route. Do not compare two color-change numbers without the information the reviewer needs to establish their comparability.

Where an unexposed reference is used, keep its identity and condition connected to the exposed specimen. If photographs illustrate a change, retain the stated viewing conditions and the original assessment record. A photo can locate the feature without replacing a required measurement.

Q-Lab’s standards FAQ explains that some standards define an exposure method without providing every pass/fail requirement. Keep the actual accepting specification and owner visible. Naming a familiar standard does not by itself establish that the sticker passed the buyer’s required appearance or readability criterion.

Keep screening and service-life prediction distinct

Q-Lab distinguishes screening or qualification from correlation and prediction, and its FAQ rejects a general conversion from accelerated hours to outdoor years. A controlled comparison can be useful without supplying a calendar-life promise. Preserve what the test actually supports rather than multiplying its hours by a convenient factor.

If a provider proposes a service-life interpretation, ask what relevant outdoor data, construction and failure mode support that particular interpretation. Keep the qualified explanation with the actual installed-use profile. Do not transfer a model for another material or climate to the sticker merely because the report uses the same instrument name.

For a short promotional use, a bounded comparison may be sufficient for the owner’s decision. For another use, evidence gaps may remain. The appropriate choice depends on the actual requirement and available support; demanding the largest hour total is not a substitute for identifying it.

Close the evidence gap before changing the construction

Accept a comparison only within its supported scope. Return missing specimen or cycle information for clarification. If the reports cannot be reconciled, ask the responsible technical parties what further comparison would be meaningful. Keep the decision pending rather than treating incompatible results as equal or automatically choosing a heavier protective layer.

The matte/gloss guide covers the separate appearance tradeoff, while the wet-use guide and rub-evidence guide address different exposures. Passing one does not settle the others.

For a die-cut sticker project, send artwork and critical features, final size and quantity, installed surface and orientation, proposed construction and any identified report through a weathering-evidence enquiry. State whether the unresolved decision concerns a print comparison or a proposed use claim. Any sample or review availability must be confirmed under the project-specific policy; this guide establishes no ZC test facility or outdoor-life guarantee.
