Holographic Artwork: Separate Pattern Placement from Reflection

Updated October 22, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Rank information before adding effects
  2. Turn the composition into an area map
  3. Review the opacity plan separately from the artwork composite
  4. Examine a physical reference under useful conditions
  5. Keep cutting, protection and security claims separate
  6. Release an effect that the buyer can describe again
  7. Decide whether the material motif must land in one exact place

A holographic mockup places one bright prism inside a character. That attractive crop may come from the material pattern rather than the printed artwork. Separate reflection and information planning from a second decision: whether the supplier is offering that exact motif position on every finished shape, or an accepted range of pattern variation.

Before approving holographic sticker printing, divide the design into areas that may reflect and areas whose information takes priority. Then connect that map to the proposed opaque-print plan and a physical reference. The useful decision is how much of the effect the design can accept in each place, rather than whether holographic material is generally a premium option.

Rank information before adding effects

Start with the product name, brand mark and any information the responsible product owner requires. Mark which parts must be read during normal handling or shelf viewing. Treat decorative lettering separately; it can tolerate a different visual priority when the buyer deliberately accepts that choice.

Next, identify the role of reflection. A border might frame a solid central mark. Open areas around a character might provide movement. A broad reflective background might be the main decorative feature on a giveaway sticker. These are legitimate choices, but they produce different review tasks.

If every area is marked “maximum holographic effect,” the file gives no answer when reflection interferes with essential text. Resolve that conflict in the design brief. Reducing reflective coverage, enlarging a quiet information panel or changing the material direction may be more useful than asking the printer to preserve mutually competing effects.

Turn the composition into an area map

Use a marked reference to explain the appearance you intend. Keep it distinct from the production file unless the supplier asks for a particular layer or spot-color setup. A descriptive overlay is a communication aid; it does not automatically become a printable separation.

Ask the supplier how that intent would be translated into the actual print route. White or other opaque printing may be discussed for selected areas, but the result depends on the construction and process. Do not assume one universal percentage of coverage will create a particular degree of opacity.

Review the opacity plan separately from the artwork composite

A flattened mockup can hide a missing production instruction. Ask to see which areas are intended to receive opaque support and which are intended to remain open to the material effect. Check narrow borders, letter counters and small spaces where an unexpected solid area would change the composition.

Printer-specific conventions matter. Avery's published artwork guidance, for example, identifies its own method for specifying white underprinting. That demonstrates the need for an agreed convention; it is not a naming rule for every printer or a claim about the ZC Labels press route. Obtain the actual instructions before finalizing the separation.

For detailed text and white-print alignment, use the white ink and readability guide. This article controls the overall reflective composition. The text guide controls the small elements that must survive within it.

Examine a physical reference under useful conditions

A digital proof helps confirm the planned areas, wording, size and shape. It does not reproduce the movement of a physical holographic surface. Where that movement affects acceptance, ask what material reference or project sample can represent the proposed result and what differences remain.

View the reference in the orientation in which the sticker will be used. Turn it through ordinary handling angles and use the lighting conditions that matter to the project. Record the conditions and the areas that become difficult to read. A phone photograph can document an observation, but one photograph cannot establish that every angle is acceptable.

If the product must also be photographed for an online store, include that task in the review. The best physical shelf effect may be difficult to describe in a single image. Decide whether a quieter central panel, several honest photographs or a less reflective construction fits the actual selling presentation.

Keep cutting, protection and security claims separate

The reflective material still needs a workable finished shape. Check whether fine projecting points, internal spaces or narrow borders remain appropriate for the selected delivered format. Ask about any concerns before approving artwork around a shape that later changes.

A protective layer can also change the appearance under review. Identify it as part of the proposed construction rather than approving an unprotected reference and assuming the final surface will look identical. If a change is proposed, decide which observations need repeating.

Decorative holographic appearance does not establish tamper evidence, authentication or anti-counterfeit performance. Those are separate requirements needing their own construction and evidence. Avoid adding a security promise to a decorative project because the surface resembles a security label.

Release an effect that the buyer can describe again

Keep the accepted artwork revision, final size, reflective-area map and the reference used for the physical appearance decision together. Record any areas allowed to vary and any information whose readability remains a release condition. If the buyer instead needs a stable white background, the clear versus white material guide provides a related alternative discussion.

Decide whether the material motif must land in one exact place

A presentation mockup can place a bright prism neatly inside a character's face. If that prism belongs to the purchased material pattern rather than the printed artwork, ask whether the proposed converting route controls its position relative to the cut. Do not let the crop in a sales image become an unstated promise for every finished piece.

The distinction is real but product-specific. Avery Dennison's Confetti Film data sheet describes its pattern repeat and seam location in converted material. Those details belong to that film; they are not specifications for every holographic sticker or evidence that ZC uses it. Ask for the proposed material's own repeat, seam and layout information.

Record which features are printed and which come from the underlying material. Then mark the buyer's requirement: a general moving background with permitted variation, or a specific material motif at a controlled position. If exact placement is essential, leave that requirement pending until the supplier confirms a feasible route and a suitable reference. A decorative film description alone does not confirm registered motif placement.

Wider identified material sample Pattern scale and visible boundaries

If ordinary pattern variation is acceptable, approve an honest range of appearances rather than one unusually attractive piece. If it is unacceptable, compare a less directional pattern, a quieter opaque information area or artwork that carries the critical motif itself. Each can preserve the design's purpose while changing how much depends on the substrate.

Keep this decision separate from print-to-cut alignment and information readability. A pattern can vary acceptably while the printed brand still needs its agreed position. Conversely, perfectly positioned text does not make an unapproved material crop acceptable. Retain both decisions in the finish brief so the next order does not inherit an impossible mockup.

Send final artwork, dimensions, quantities, an identified material reference and whether pattern-crop variation is acceptable in a holographic-label enquiry. State separately any exact motif-placement requirement for feasibility review.
