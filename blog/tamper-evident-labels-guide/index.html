Tamper-Evident Labels: Map the Openings the Seal Covers

Published 2026-09-23. Updated October 26, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

A seal bridges the front flap, yet the carton also has a separate opening at the other end. Decide which routes the proposed seal covers before treating its visible presence as a package-wide promise. One clear opening indicator can answer a useful question without answering every access question.

What Does Tamper Evident Mean?

A tamper-evident label is intended to leave visible evidence when the protected opening is disturbed. It is not automatically tamper-proof: a seal may discourage casual opening or reveal it, but it cannot guarantee that nobody can access the contents. A plain closure sticker may hold a flap shut without showing a clear change after removal. Specify the evidence you expect before choosing a label.

Which Layers Create the Opening Evidence?

A typical self-adhesive label starts with a printable face material, print or protective finish, adhesive and a release liner that is removed during application. That basic stack alone does not prove tamper evidence. Depending on the intended result, the proposed construction may add a destructible face, perforation or pattern-transfer layer. Ask for a cross-section or material specification for the exact sample, not a generic security label name.

Part of the sealQuestion to check
Face material and printDoes the label tear, split or stay intact when lifted?
Adhesive and contacted surfaceDoes it bond to both sides of the opening without unwanted edge lift?
Perforation or transfer feature, if usedIs the change visible after normal opening and after an attempted peel?
Release liner before applicationCan operators peel and place the seal consistently?

The answer changes with glass, metal, plastic, coated board, storage and opening force. Do not infer a particular layer stack or regulated performance from an illustration or product photo.

Begin With the Opening Path

Place the unopened package on the table and open it normally. Watch the first point that moves. A jar lid rotates. A tuck carton flap lifts. A mailer tears along a strip. A clamshell separates at an edge. The seal should bridge the actual opening path rather than decorate a nearby area.

For a jar, a narrow strip may travel from the lid top across the edge to the container. For a box, a circular or shaped seal may cross two flaps. If the seal can be removed without disturbing the closure, it is not demonstrating the intended event.

PackageSeal challengeTest focus
Glass jar with metal lidTwo materials plus a sharp edgeBond on lid and glass, fold and opening tear
Plastic jarCurvature and lower-energy surfaceEdge lift and removal evidence
Coated cartonVarnish and paper fiber behaviorSeal bond, fiber tear and print damage
Kraft mailerTexture, dust and shipping abrasionContact, rubbing and delivery condition

Review the complete package before the label location

Assemble the actual carton, jar or other package with its normal components. Identify the opening the customer is intended to use and any other ordinary detachable or opening features. Include sleeves, lids, inserts and outer packaging only where they affect the stated task. Use the package owner's information; do not invent a covert-attack test or claim that a simple inspection finds every possible access route.

Photograph the package from the relevant sides. Mark the proposed seal position and name the opening it bridges. A front photograph may be sufficient to discuss artwork while leaving the bottom flap invisible. Keep that limitation explicit rather than enlarging the front sticker to create a more secure-looking presentation.

The illustration shows one fictional gift-carton opening before and after a paper bridge is torn. It explains the observed point only. It does not establish that the bottom, side seams or any other route is protected, and it is not a tamper-performance test.

Give each route an explicit status

Create a short coverage map. Name what the seal is intended to indicate, which component carries that function and what is outside the proposed scope. Unknown coverage should remain unknown until the responsible packaging owner resolves it. Do not label every unsealed area a defect when the actual requirement may be narrower.

Intended customer openingIdentified seal or other featureWhat visible change is required?

Removable sleeve or outer packSeparate role if relevantWhich layer is being observed?

This is a scope record, not a security rating or a statement that the package meets a standard. If a product, customer or market requires a particular protective feature or assessment, obtain the actual requirement and qualified interpretation. A sticker supplier should not infer that obligation from the name of the product.

Match the construction review to the covered route

Once a route is identified, define the required visible change on the actual contacted surfaces. The label may cross coated board on both sides, or a lid and container with different materials. Record the position, dimensions and application method before reviewing a proposed construction. A result at one surface pair cannot approve a different route by association.

For transfer patterns and fragmenting faces, use the VOID and destructible comparison. That focused guide covers intact references, intended changes and exception handling. Keeping those details there avoids treating this coverage map as a second construction-comparison article.

An alternative to adding more seals may be a different owner-approved package arrangement that makes the intended opening clearer. More pieces can add application and inspection work; a broader visual footprint can also make normal opening awkward. Compare the actual requirement, packing work and customer use instead of assuming that the largest seal offers the broadest supported claim.

Preserve the boundary in the release record

Retain the package reference, coverage map, proposed seal construction and accepted observation scope together. If a new carton has another opening arrangement, compare the affected routes before reusing the earlier sign-off. A new illustration on an unchanged seal and a change in package access are different revisions.

Keep the receiving instruction consistent with that boundary. A receiver should know what the visible feature is intended to communicate and where an ambiguous observation goes for review. It should not be asked to prove product safety, authenticity or complete chain of custody.

The die-cut sticker page supports a supplied-shape discussion without establishing security capability. Send a package-opening coverage brief with all relevant package views, marked opening routes, intended indication, surface descriptions, artwork, dimensions and quantities. State which routes are covered, excluded or unresolved. That makes the next review precise enough to explain what the seal is actually being asked to show.
