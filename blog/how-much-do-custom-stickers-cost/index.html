How Much Do Custom Stickers Cost?

A practical pricing checklist for B2B custom sticker inquiries.

By ZC Labels Production Team · Reviewed for B2B sticker sourcing.

Custom sticker pricing is the result of a specification, not a fixed number attached to the word "sticker." Two designs at the same size can require different material, cutline, print coverage, packing and proof work. A useful quote explains what finished product is being priced.

The Eight Inputs Behind a Quote

InputWhy it changes the quoteUseful buyer detail
Artwork countMultiple designs affect setup, sorting and packingQuantity required for each design
Size and shapeChanges material use and cutline reviewFinal dimensions, not only "small" or "large"
FormatDie-cut, kiss-cut, sheet and roll structures differHow the stickers should be supplied
MaterialPaper, white film, clear and holographic directions differSurface and use condition
FinishMatte, gloss and special effects add different stepsVisual priority and handling need
QuantityAffects setup allocation and total materialTest order and likely reorder range
PackingCounting, sorting, bagging and cards add handlingFinished packs, not only loose piece count
DeliveryDestination and deadline affect shipping choicesCountry, postal code and required window

Why a "Price Per Sticker" Can Mislead

Unit price is useful only when the compared quotes describe the same product. A lower number may exclude individual bagging, backing cards, roll direction, proof work or freight. One quote may count individual stickers while another counts finished retail packs.

Ask suppliers to restate the specification beside the price: artwork count, dimensions, material, finish, quantity, packing and delivery basis. That turns a price list into a decision.

How to Reduce Cost Without Damaging the Product

Start with the requirement that matters most. If the sticker is an indoor packaging seal, an expensive special effect may not improve the job. If shelf presentation is the priority, removing every finishing choice may weaken the product.

  • Standardize sizes across related designs where practical
  • Choose one material and finish for a first test range
  • Separate "must have" packing from optional presentation
  • Provide clean artwork to reduce avoidable revision rounds
  • Compare the smallest useful test order with the expected reorder

A Better Quote Request

Instead of asking "How much for 1,000 stickers?", describe the finished result: "1,000 matte logo stickers, 50 mm wide, individual die-cut pieces for indoor mailer boxes, one artwork, bulk packed, shipping to [destination]." If the material is uncertain, say so and describe the surface.
