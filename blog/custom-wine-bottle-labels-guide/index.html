Wine Labels: Keep Vintage Changes Tied to Approved Artwork

Updated October 26, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

The new vintage uses the same design family, so the old front and back files are opened for a quick edit. Stop before treating a changed year as the whole release. Identify every field the wine owner has authorized to change, and which approved references still control the remaining copy.

Build the Dieline Around the Bottle, Not the Rendering

Measure several final bottles and mark the usable front, back and neck areas. If the bottle has a strong mold seam, decide whether the front label centers between seams. Apply paper prototypes and view the bottle upright, in a case and on a shelf.

For front-and-back sets, define spacing and alignment. For wraps, decide where the seam or overlap lands. A millimeter that looks trivial on screen can become obvious when twelve bottles stand in a carton.

Premium Does Not Mean Empty

Original vineyard rows, grape clusters, local plants, birds, hills, cellar tools and geometric maps can give wine packaging real character. Avoiding a copied château crest does not require a plain white rectangle with one word in the center.

Decide what the illustration contributes. It might separate grape varieties, distinguish a reserve line or express landscape. Keep the producer name and wine type readable. The artwork should reward a closer look without hiding the buying decision.

Paper Texture Has a Real Handling Cost

An uncoated or textured paper can feel warm and tactile. It may also react differently to condensation, wet ice service, abrasion or adhesive squeeze-out. A protected construction can improve resistance and change the feel. There is no universal premium material.

Test labeled bottles through the actual route: application, case packing, cellar or refrigerated storage, transport, retail handling and service. If the wine enters an ice bucket, say so before material selection, not after the first complaint.

Plan for Vintage and Market Changes

Vintage, alcohol content, importer details, geographic statements, barcodes and awards can change. Separate stable artwork from controlled variable fields. Name files so the vintage and market are visible before anyone opens them.

Do not print an award medallion, appellation symbol or sustainability mark without permission and evidence. Original visual authority is welcome; invented official authority is not.

Build a field-change sheet before editing

Keep the old released artwork as a reference, then create a named revision for the new order. List the front, back and any supplementary pieces separately. Record the product and destination alongside the owner-approved wording. The printer should not infer a market version or wine description from the appearance of a familiar bottle.

For each field, mark retain, change or awaiting owner confirmation. This applies to visible identity and any variable or market-specific content included in the project. This guide does not decide wine-label requirements, permitted claims or approval obligations. Those decisions remain with the responsible wine business and its qualified advisers.

The two vintages in the illustration are fictional file references. They demonstrate a change review, not a recommendation to alter every statement annually or a claim that either pictured label is a complete consumer label. A plausible year on a bottle is not proof that the associated wording is authorized.

Keep front and back revisions connected

A change on the front may affect wording or identity on the back. It may also leave the back unchanged. Record the actual decision rather than forcing both into a new version merely for neatness. The important part is that the released pair is explicit and the packing team is not asked to match it from memory.

Existing stock needs its own decision. Do not assume that a familiar label can be over-stickered or used up with the new product. If a correction route is proposed, review it as a separate authorized packaging task. The printer cannot decide that a small old field is unimportant because the new label looks similar.

Compare the exported release with the change list

Check the final output against both the approved new text and the identified old reference. A file comparison can help locate differences, but the owner still decides whether each difference is intended. Adobe's PDF comparison instructions describe a review aid, not a wine-content validation service.

Inspect unchanged areas as well as changed ones. A layout adjustment can move a previously accepted line, and a font or export change can affect a field that nobody intended to edit. Then print at final size and check the paired layouts on the current bottle. A correct text comparison does not settle fit or reading conditions.

The practical alternative to revising a whole range at once is to release only the combinations whose source content and physical references are settled, if that works for the order and supplier. Compare the extra version and stock-control work with the risk of treating unfinished copy as accepted. Do not invent delivery or cost advantages.

For the general repeat-order handoff, use the reorder checklist. The roll label page supports the printing-format discussion.

Send a vintage artwork change brief with old and proposed front/back files, a field-change list, owner-approved copy by destination, current bottle photos, quantities per version and old-stock questions. That makes the next proof a controlled release rather than a year changed in a familiar picture.
