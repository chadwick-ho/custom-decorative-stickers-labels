Sticker Sheets Sticking Together: Check the Packed Stack

Published November 16, 2026.

By ZC Labels · B2B procurement guide · Sources and editorial approach

In this guide
  1. Locate the problem before changing the specification
  2. Use a representative packed sample
  3. Compare another pack only if it answers the same handling need

Review a packed stack when a sticker sheet order must be picked one sheet at a time. Open a representative pack as the receiving or packing team will open it. Check that adjacent sheets separate without changing the printed face, backing or wanted sticker pieces. Ask for a revised pack proposal when normal picking leaves an unresolved contact problem.

Locate the problem before changing the specification

Record what happens when a complete sheet is picked. Two sheets may come away together and separate without damage. Another pair may separate with a mark on the backing or a visibly altered printed face. A sticker piece may stay attached to the sheet above it. These observations give the supplier different evidence to investigate.

ACTEGA's guidance for printed sheet piles discusses sticking between sheets and unwanted transfer to a contact surface. The explanation concerns its printing and coating context. Use the distinction to describe what happened, then have the supplier review the actual sticker construction and handling history before assigning a cause. ACTEGA guidance on printed sheets sticking together.

If the trouble begins when a user peels one sticker from an otherwise cleanly separated sheet, use the kiss-cut separation guide to record that different action. A curled sheet that catches in a sleeve needs a whole-sheet fit and flatness review.

Use a representative packed sample

Give the supplier the construction, layout revision, bag or bundle count, face orientation and any separators used in the order proposal. Agree where and when to review the sample using the relevant handling or storage conditions. The check is most useful when the sample has the same contact arrangement the buyer will open. If it represents only a short stack or a different wrapper, record that limit.

Compare another pack only if it answers the same handling need

A supplier might propose smaller bundles, a changed orientation or a suitable separator. Ask how the change affects the work of unpacking, individual sheet counts and retail presentation. Review the revised arrangement using the same pick task. If the evidence is limited to a trial pack, record what still needs agreement for production acceptance and receiving.

Send the affected packing or intended pick sequence, quantity of sheets per bundle, artwork and construction references, and any paired photographs. Ask which sample can represent the proposed stack and which packing changes can be reviewed. ZC custom sticker sheets and project sample policy.
